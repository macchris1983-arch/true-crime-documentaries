TITLE
The Axeman of New Orleans: Who Wrote the Letter From Hell? (1918–1919)

DESCRIPTION
In March 1919, a letter signed "The Axman" told New Orleans that any house with a jazz band playing would be spared. The city played. No one was killed that night. It is the most famous thing about the case, and it has never been tied to the person who attacked these families.

This film goes back to the record: the newspapers of 1918 and 1919, and the court cases that followed. It shows where the record splits. The dates of the attacks, the date the letter ran, the number of victims, the "last victim," and the Los Angeles ending most people have heard.

This film does not name a killer. Frank Jordano and his father, Iorlando, were convicted in 1919 on the identification of the key witness, who later took it back; the Louisiana Supreme Court overturned the convictions in 1920 and the charges were dropped. No conviction for these attacks has ever stood. Everyone named here who was suspected or charged is presumed innocent. A newspaper report is not proof.

Where sources disagree, the film says so on screen: the Maggio attack (May 22 or 23, 1918), the Cortimiglia attack (March 9 or 10, 1919), and the letter (dated March 13, printed Sunday, March 16, 1919).
