TITLE
The Oklahoma City Bomber Was Already in Jail — and John Doe #2 Was Never There (1995)

DESCRIPTION
At 10:20 on the morning of April 19, 1995, an Oklahoma state trooper pulled over a yellow Mercury with no license plate. The driver had a loaded pistol under his jacket. He was booked into the Noble County jail in Perry on misdemeanor charges. Two days later, the FBI ran his name and found him there.

This film follows the case from the record: the rear axle and its vehicle identification number, the Ryder rental in Junction City, Kansas, the two sketches, and the second suspect the whole country searched for. In June 1995 the Justice Department said John Doe Number Two was an Army private who had rented a truck at the same body shop a day apart from the bomber. He had nothing to do with the bombing.

Timothy McVeigh was convicted in 1997 and executed in 2001. Terry Nichols was convicted in federal court in 1997 and in Oklahoma state court in 2004 and is serving life. Michael Fortier, who knew of the plan, testified and was sentenced to twelve years. Where accounts differ, such as how McVeigh's name first reached the FBI, the film shows both on screen. A report is not proof.

168 people were killed, 19 of them children. This film shows no victims and no injured people.
