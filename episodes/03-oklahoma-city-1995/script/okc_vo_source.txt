=== CHUNK 00a ===
At twenty past ten on a Wednesday morning, a state trooper in Oklahoma pulled over a yellow car with no license plate.
He found a pistol under the driver's jacket.
He booked the driver into a small county jail.
For the next two days, the FBI went looking for that man.
He was already in a cell.
And the second man the whole country searched for
was never there at all.

=== CHUNK 00c ===
This is the Oklahoma City bombing.
April nineteenth, nineteen ninety-five.
A hundred and sixty-eight people killed.
This is not a mystery about who did it. A jury decided that.
It is a story about how the case was found,
and about a second suspect the whole country searched for,
who turned out to be someone else.
I'm going to show you the record.
Federal footage, the court files, and the government's own reports.

=== CHUNK 01 ===
At two minutes past nine, a rental truck exploded outside the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building.
About a third of the building came down.
More than three hundred buildings nearby were damaged or destroyed.
The FBI counts a hundred and sixty-eight people killed.
Nineteen of them were children.
Several hundred more were hurt.
The footage you are seeing was filmed by the Oklahoma National Guard.
It shows the search, in the rain, in the hours and days after.
I will not show you the people who were hurt.
That is a choice.

=== CHUNK 02 ===
On April twentieth, investigators found a heavy piece of the truck.
Part of a rear axle.
It carried a vehicle identification number.
That number led to a Ryder rental, from a body shop in Junction City, Kansas.
The truck had been reserved under the name Robert Kling.
A deposit on April fifteenth. Picked up on the seventeenth.
The people at the body shop described the man who rented it.
Their descriptions became two sketches.
John Doe Number One.
And John Doe Number Two.

=== CHUNK 03 ===
Go back to the nineteenth.
About ten twenty in the morning, on Interstate thirty-five, north of Oklahoma City.
Trooper Charles Hanger saw a yellow Mercury with no tag on the back.
He pulled it over between mile markers two oh two and two oh three.
The driver handed him a Michigan license.
Timothy James McVeigh.
When the trooper reached toward a bulge under his jacket, the driver said, "I have a gun."
A forty-five caliber pistol, with a round in the chamber.
The dispatcher found no warrants and no record.
The trooper booked him into the Noble County jail in Perry
on misdemeanor charges: the gun, the missing plate, no proof of insurance.
He did not know what the man was.
Nobody did yet.

=== CHUNK 04 ===
On April twenty-first, the sketch of John Doe Number One was everywhere.
How the name came back to the FBI depends on which account you read.
The FBI's history says employees at a local motel supplied it.
A trial chronology says a former co-worker recognized the face.
Both may be true.
Either way, agents ran the name through the FBI's national records.
It came back with an answer nobody expected.
Timothy McVeigh was in custody, in Perry, Oklahoma.
He had been there since the morning of the bombing.
He was due in front of a local judge that day, on the misdemeanor charges.
Federal agents reached him first.
The same day, in Herington, Kansas, a man named Terry Nichols turned himself in to police.

=== CHUNK 05 ===
That left the second sketch.
A stocky man, dark hair, a ball cap.
The FBI put it out across the country.
Tips poured in. At least a dozen men were picked up.
Then, in June, the Justice Department said it had found him.
He was an Army private, from the base nearby.
He had come into the same body shop with another soldier to rent a truck of their own.
A day apart from the man in the first sketch.
The mechanic had remembered a real face, on the wrong day.
The private had nothing to do with the bombing.
The government said so in June, nineteen ninety-five, and again in court in nineteen ninety-seven.
A county grand jury in Oklahoma went over it for eighteen months
and came to the same conclusion.
But the sketch had already been everywhere.
And a face that has been on every wall
does not leave when the paperwork says it should.
To this day, there are people who believe John Doe Number Two was real, and got away.
The record says he was a soldier renting a truck.

=== CHUNK 06 ===
The trial was moved to Denver, so the jury would not be drawn from the city that was hit.
On June second, nineteen ninety-seven, a jury convicted Timothy McVeigh on all eleven counts.
Eleven days later, it voted for death.
Terry Nichols was tried separately.
That December, a jury convicted him of conspiracy and involuntary manslaughter,
and acquitted him of using a weapon of mass destruction.
He was sentenced to life.
In two thousand four, an Oklahoma jury convicted him of more than a hundred and sixty murders.
It could not agree on death.
Michael Fortier, who knew of the plan and warned no one, testified for the government.
He was sentenced to twelve years.

=== CHUNK 07 ===
The case had one more turn.
In May, two thousand one, days before the execution date,
the Justice Department said the FBI had failed to give the defense
more than four thousand pages of documents before trial.
The execution was postponed by a month.
In June, a federal judge refused to delay it again.
On June eleventh, two thousand one, Timothy McVeigh was executed.
Survivors and families watched on closed-circuit television in Oklahoma City.

=== CHUNK 08 ===
So here is what the record holds.
A truck. An axle with a number on it. A body shop. A motel.
A trooper who stopped a car because it had no plate.
And here is what was added.
A second man in a ball cap, who was a soldier on the wrong day.
And a belief, still told, that the case is missing someone.
The courts convicted three men, for different parts of it.
The Army private in the second sketch was cleared, and is owed that.
Where the memorial now stands, there is a field of a hundred and sixty-eight empty chairs.
This film is drawn from federal records, court filings and the reporting of the time.
A report is not proof.
And the arrest that held him
was made by a trooper looking at the back of a car.
